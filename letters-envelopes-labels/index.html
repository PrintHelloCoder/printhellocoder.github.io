Print Letters, Envelopes and Mailing Labels

Follow a recipient’s information through printing, assembly, and postal handoff.

By Veronica Smith · Updated October 7, 2026 · 7 min read · 6 references

A service that prints letters, addresses envelopes or labels, folds the contents, inserts them, and prepares the mailing is commonly described as a lettershop, mail-house, or print-and-mail service. The scope varies by provider, so name the required operations when requesting a quote.

The central production problem is matching. The correct letter must reach the correct envelope, with the correct enclosures, in the correct quantity. This guide explains how to structure that job whether you handle a small run internally or work with a specialist provider.

1 Define the complete mailing pack

List every component of the finished piece. A simple pack might contain one letter and one envelope. A more complex pack might include a personalized letter, a brochure selected by segment, and a reply item. State whether the number of pages or enclosures can vary by recipient.

Choose the envelope before finalizing the address placement. A window envelope can reduce the need to print a second address, but the folded letter must keep the delivery block visible and correctly positioned. A non-window envelope creates a separate addressing task and a matching requirement.

Record finished dimensions, paper, envelope type, printed sides, fold style, and sealing requirements. Ask the provider to confirm that the proposed pack is compatible with its equipment and the intended postal service. A design that looks simple in a PDF can be awkward to fold or insert at volume.

Include the expected output in the quote request: loose printed components, assembled sealed envelopes, or a mailing handed to the postal operator. Those are materially different services.

2 Prepare the address and document data

Keep a stable recipient ID throughout the job. Use separate fields for names, organization, address lines, city, region, postal code, and country as appropriate. Preserve the source meaning while formatting the output for the destination’s postal requirements.

Canada Post’s addressing guidance is the relevant reference for Canadian formatting. [2] Avoid inserting decoration or marketing text into the delivery block simply because there is space. Postal information needs to remain clear and recognizable.

Check duplicates, missing delivery details, and unusual multi-line addresses before composition. A duplicate may be intentional if two people at one company should each receive a pack, but that decision should be explicit. Do not automatically collapse different recipients merely because the address matches.

Freeze the approved dataset for the job. If the list is changed after envelopes have been printed, regenerate or reconcile the affected components before assembly. Two valid files from different moments can create an invalid combined mailing.

3 Merge the letter and the envelope

Use the same approved source and recipient IDs for both outputs. Microsoft’s envelope mail-merge documentation describes a common software route. [1] For a window-envelope workflow, proof the address on the folded letter inside the actual envelope rather than relying solely on the on-screen template.

Check page boundaries in the letter merge. A long address or paragraph can push the signature onto a new page and alter the expected pack size. If the job requires exactly one sheet per recipient, define how overflows are handled before the merge is approved.

Suppress optional empty lines deliberately without removing necessary separation. Inspect records with and without company names, apartment or suite details, and long recipient names. Keep a minimum readable type size rather than shrinking every difficult address until it fits.

For duplex letters, identify whether blank backs must be retained to preserve document boundaries. An output optimizer that removes blank pages without understanding the pack can shift subsequent recipients onto the wrong sides.

4 Decide between direct addressing and labels

Directly addressing an envelope avoids a label application step, but requires suitable equipment and envelope handling. Labels can be useful for smaller or mixed runs, provided the label size, material, placement, and adhesion fit the job.

Count the labor as well as the consumables. Manually applying labels creates opportunities for skewed placement, wrinkles, duplicates, and mismatches. A small job may still be manageable, but the process should include a clear sequence and a completed-piece count.

Test a label on the actual envelope stock. Confirm that it remains legible and attached after ordinary handling. Do not cover important postal areas or place it where a fold or seam makes the address difficult to read.

If you use a dedicated database-linked label workflow, read the Brother guide for the data and preview principles. The operational controls apply beyond one brand: correct field mapping and physical testing matter regardless of the software used.

5 Fold and insert without losing the match

For manual assembly, work in manageable batches and keep the letter and envelope stacks in a known order. Establish a clear orientation for pickup and turning. Avoid mixing unfinished and completed packs on the same surface.

For automated inserting, ask the provider how it identifies document boundaries and verifies the match. Variable page counts or segment-specific enclosures may require additional controls. The provider should explain how exceptions are diverted and reconciled.

Never treat a matching sequence as sufficient after a jam or interrupted run. Check the next recipient in each component stream before resuming. If a letter is damaged, identify and replace the complete affected pack according to the approved recovery procedure.

Use a manifest to track expected packs, completed packs, rejected components, and reprints. The final reconciliation should account for every eligible recipient exactly as intended. Sheet counts alone cannot establish that the right contents reached the right envelopes.

6 Proof the physical pack and postal suitability

Inspect the sealed or seal-ready sample, not only the individual printed parts. Check that the letter fits, the fold is clean, the address is visible, and no required content is hidden. Confirm the final weight and dimensions using the intended materials.

Canada Post’s machineable-mail guidance is a useful production reference for Canadian Personalized Mail. [3] Eligibility depends on the actual piece and current service requirements. Have the provider confirm the applicable specifications rather than assuming a familiar envelope size is automatically suitable.

The PDF Association’s print-file guidance can help with the prepress stage, while the physical proof verifies the assembly stage. [5] Each answers different questions. A correct PDF does not establish that an envelope window will stay aligned after folding.

Research on mailing design also distinguishes the envelope’s opening role from the contents’ later role. [4] Use that distinction when reviewing the pack: the outside should help the recipient understand the communication, and the inside should make the proposed action clear.

7 An illustrative reconciliation example

Suppose a fictional job begins with 350 eligible recipients. During proofing, five addresses are held for correction, leaving 345 approved packs. Production creates 345 letters and 345 addressed envelopes. Seven letters are damaged during handling and replaced.

The replacement letters do not increase the intended mailing quantity. The damaged originals must be removed from the assembly area and recorded. The final pack count should remain 345, with each approved recipient represented once. The five held records remain outside the released job unless a separately approved supplementary batch is created.

StageCountMeaning
Initial eligible list350Before address holds
Held records5Excluded from this release
Approved mailing345Expected finished packs
Replacement letters7Replace damaged originals, not extra recipients
Final packs345Reconciled to approved IDs

This simple example shows why attempts, sheets, replacements, and finished pieces should be recorded separately. Combining them into one “printed” total conceals the information needed to verify the mailing.

8 Request a useful lettershop quote

Send the provider a concise specification with quantity, document sizes, page counts, sides, stock, envelope choice, fold, personalization fields, enclosure rules, and destination geography. Include whether your data is ready for production or needs cleanup.

Ask for a breakdown of setup, composition, proofs, printing, folding, inserting, addressing, postal preparation, and postage. Clarify turnaround after approval, the correction process, and what evidence will be supplied after mailing.

Discuss how source data will be transferred, who may access it, and when job files are removed according to your agreement. Avoid sending unnecessary customer information merely because it exists in the CRM export. The provider needs the fields required to produce and account for the job.

Keep a final sample and the approved manifest with the handoff record. Use the actual production experience to improve the next specification. The campaign guide connects these production steps to marketing approval and follow-up.
